## 3. Patterns of critique

**What Karp attacks:**

1. **Derivative consumer tech.** Post-Cold-War SV abandoned hard problems (defense, intelligence, infrastructure) for consumer dopamine. Recurring symbol: eToys.com. AI version: "AI slop."

2. **Horizontal infrastructure without vertical worldview.** Palantir's whole thesis: generic compute (AWS, Azure, Google Cloud) cannot substitute for typed, domain-specific Ontology grounded in operational reality. *"The route for us to something universal was made possible by an obsessive focus on the specific."*

3. **Performative ESG / theatrical politics in tech.** *"When there is no yardstick, no willingness to measure and quantify... we must be willing to expose our purported shepherds as frauds."*

4. **Naive sovereignty arguments that ignore real procurement.** Western governments are Palantir's customers, not adversaries. The "consumer rights to data" frame is, to him, the wrong frame for serious AI architecture.

5. **Founders who don't ship into hard domains.** Foundry headline: *"The Ontology-Powered Operating System for the Modern Enterprise"* — not a platform, not a protocol, an operating system.

**What Karp endorses:**

1. **Typed, reality-grounded ontologies as the moat.** *"The Ontology is based firmly in reality — there is, here, a dialectic between ground truth, tribal knowledge, and enhancements."* (Q1 2026)

2. **Aesthetic/cultural discipline over headcount growth.** *"The enemy of art is the absence of limitations."*

3. **Hard customers as validation signal.** DoD trust = real; consumer apps = probably not load-bearing.

4. **Western civilizational commitment as architecture.** The "Soft Belief" half of his book's subtitle is non-negotiable.

## 4. Adjacency to HPI's concerns

**On the Ontology as the product:** Palantir's central claim. Foundry is "The Ontology-Powered Operating System." The Ontology is a typed object graph (Aircraft, Soldier, Invoice, PatentApplication) with properties, actions, relationships — defined before data flows. AI agents in AIP query the Ontology, execute actions against it, return results. **Critically: the Ontology is enterprise-defined, not user-defined.** Customers conform their data to Palantir's typed objects. This is the architectural opposite of HPI's "individual sovereign substrate."

**On AI agent infrastructure:** AIP agents do not have persistent memory outside the Ontology — stateless queries against typed objects. *Architecturally close to HPI's "agents borrow scoped views via tokens"* — but locus of control is institutional, not individual.

**On user data sovereignty:** Unusual position. Palantir's privacy contracts are strict — customer data never leaves customer environment. But this is *institutional* sovereignty, not *individual* sovereignty. He has attacked Google/Facebook for casual data treatment but does not advocate for individual user control of AI context.

**On hyperscalers vs. protocols:** No public approving use of "protocol." His position: the right architecture is vertical, proprietary, deeply embedded — not open. *"The future belongs not to those who style themselves as creators, or even to the merely brave, but to those who actually build something substantial and lasting."*

**Internal Viter steelman (Apr 29 2026) anticipates Karp precisely:** *"You shipped a database. We ship a worldview."* — strong validation that the simulated objection is well-calibrated.

## 6. Predicted angles of attack on HPI

### What he would endorse:

1. **The typed axiom grammar (OBL/RCG/TRU/PAT).** Closest part of HPI to Palantir's own architecture. Karp believes typed ontological systems — not generic schemas — are the only way AI operates against reality. He'd recognize OBL/RCG/TRU/PAT as doing what Palantir's typed objects do: constraining what agents can claim.

2. **"Agents must NEVER own their L3."** Maps directly to AIP. Stateless queries against typed schema, not persistent agent memory. Karp would agree on the architectural axiom; disagree on who owns the schema.

3. **Anti-hyperscaler framing.** Hyperscaler-stored AI memory is architecturally wrong — not because it violates *individual* sovereignty (he doesn't use that frame), but because it removes the Ontology from the customer's control and hands it to a commodity vendor with no domain expertise.

### Where he would attack (sharpest first):

1. **"One level too abstract."** This is the home-territory critique. He would say: OBL/RCG/TRU/PAT is a *type grammar*, not an Ontology. An Ontology specifies what objects exist in a domain (PatentApplication, Aircraft) — what they ARE, what properties, what actions, what relationships. HPI's grammar specifies the *epistemic status* of claims (obligation, recognition, trust, pattern) but doesn't commit to the *ontological content* of any domain. His likely line: *"You shipped a meta-language for claims about a world. You haven't committed to what the world contains. That's not an Ontology. It's a schema for schema."*

2. **Token enforcement is naive about adoption.** In Palantir's model, enforcement happens at the Ontology layer — agents can only query typed objects that exist; the Ontology is curated alongside the customer. HPI's tokens assume agents and principals operate in good faith within a shared schema. Karp would ask: who is the enforcement authority when an agent forges or misconfigures a token? His answer: there must be an institutional Ontology owner, not just a cryptographic issuer.

3. **No threat model.** *"Are we tough enough to scare our adversaries?"* Applied to HPI: who is the adversary? Rogue agent, compromised principal, nation-state attacker on token infrastructure? HPI's apparent threat model is *accidental* overreach (agents shouldn't own L3), not *intentional* adversarial injection. The interesting threat is adversarial.

4. **No procurement story.** *"You've built the infrastructure of a great open-source project. Now build the moat of a company."* HPI is a protocol; anyone implements it. Who is the Ontology owner that charges for access? Who maintains the typed grammar for a specific customer's substrate? Without that, HPI is a research contribution, not a business.

5. **Consumer framing applied to a serious problem.** Individual-cognitive-sovereignty is a consumer-rights frame. Real use cases (enterprise AI on sensitive operational data, defense L3) require *institutional* sovereignty. A soldier's substrate is owned by the military; a lawyer's billable-hour context by the firm. The entity that should own L3 is the institution that bears liability, not the individual.

### What he would call missing:

1. **A formal verification layer.** Palantir's Ontology has type enforcement built in. HPI's typed grammar needs a mechanism that actually validates an OBL claim is grounded in evidence at the appropriate layer. Without enforcement, the grammar is advisory.

2. **The compliance/audit story.** FedRAMP, SOC2, immutable audit. HPI's token issuance log needs to BECOME an immutable audit trail enterprises can procure against. Without this, enterprises cannot adopt.

3. **The "worldview" layer.** His deepest objection. Palantir's Ontology doesn't just type data — it encodes a worldview about what matters in a domain. HPI's typed grammar is epistemically neutral. *A protocol that can represent any cognitive substrate but commits to none is not an Ontology. It's a meta-Ontology. Meta-Ontologies are AI slop with better grammar.*
